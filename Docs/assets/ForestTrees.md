# Forest trees

The valley's trees are generated by `RegionTreePalette` when a region loads, one shared set per biome.
Woodland has six evergreens (a broad fir, a slender fir, a tiered spruce, a weeping spruce, a tall pine and an
open-crowned pine) and three broadleaf forms (rounded alder, slender birch, spreading woodland crown);
`RegionTreePalette.Variant` keeps woodland mostly evergreen, with broadleaf groves in stable Perlin patches.
Mountains use only the six evergreens. Tropical uses seven forms: tall and leaning coconut palms, a short fan
palm, an umbrella crown, a tall rainforest crown, a low coastal broadleaf and a low pandanus crown. Coasts
favour palms, while inland tropical stands favour broad canopy trees. These names describe art families, not
surveyed species distributions. The saved tree height, deterministic variant, width and yaw create individual
variation. Clearing consumes the same random stream as surviving trees, so removing one patch never changes
the surrounding forest.

`ForestPopulation` fills ESA WorldCover tree/mangrove habitat with irregular 8 m candidates in temperate
regions and 6 m candidates in tropical regions.
Broad stand variation, smaller gaps and a minority of younger trees keep the canopy uneven; mapped fields,
towns and water stay open. Steep slopes thin out, and roads and settlement gardens reserve crown clearance.
The scatter runs in Burst. The shared saved-tree safety limit is 800,000 rather than the old 120,000 cap.
Desert scrub remains sparse and uses two palm forms. All biome palettes use six-triangle distance trees.

Saved `forestVersion = 2` records the density pass separately from terrain generation. Opening an older
mapped region appends trees once using its saved habitat/heightfield. Original indices, positions, heights,
removal flags and terrain remain intact. Removed roots reserve 32 m, disturbance reserves two source cells,
and the game protects airport bounds and public-road corridors. Procedural generator 1–3 saves retain their
original population. The larger snapshots use the existing save format with a bounded 128 MiB read limit.
Version 2 adds denser tropical stands on forested volcanic slopes and low reef islands; a version 1
temperate forest only updates its version marker and keeps its population. Tropical slope thinning accounts
for the source crop's horizontal/vertical adaptation, with a separate hard cliff cutoff. Roots remain above
the wash zone and mapped water stays empty. Fully forested tropical downloads that exceed the tree budget
sample candidates uniformly across the entire region rather than leaving the last rows empty.
`ForestQuery` keeps a weak, disposable 100 m spatial index for construction footprints, brush previews and
ray picking; removals are read live and appended/replaced lists invalidate the index.

Every tree is a solid shape first. Submesh 0 is opaque: the trunk and the crown volumes (stacked drooping
tiers for firs and spruces, rounded leaf or needle masses for pines and broadleaf crowns, frond strips for
palms), with real normals and the crown's own shading (darker inside and underneath, lighter at the tips) in
the vertex colours. Submesh 1 is alpha-tested cards over those volumes: boughs folded into a ridge for
conifers, leaf sprays for broadleaf crowns. There are four levels (`RegionTreePalette.Levels`): the two near
levels carry the cards, the two far levels are the opaque volumes alone (about 40–70 and 5–24 triangles).
A tree therefore keeps its outline, colour and lighting from the closest view to the whole-region one, and no
level depends on a baked picture. The cards only vary brightness: `TreeCanopy.shader` divides out the atlas's
mean colour (`_TexMean`), so the near and far levels of a tree match. Conifer boughs reuse the authored
branch atlas (`ConiferBranches.png`, via the library's `Needles` material); the leaf-spray atlas is drawn by
`RegionTreePalette.LeafSprays` at load, with coverage-preserving mips so far sprays do not thin out. Each
variant records its crown (`TreeVariant.crown`) for the distant shadow shapes.
`TreeMeshGenerator`/`TreeImpostorBaker` still build the six spruce/pine model prefabs and `ForestTrees.asset`
levels; the valley no longer draws those meshes, only their bark and needle materials.
`TreeLineupReview.FromCommandLine` renders every tree of a palette at every level, close, oblique and as a
mixed stand, under the New Game lighting (`Captures/TreeLineup`, with per-level triangle counts).

`ForestRenderer` uses a two-level spatial hierarchy: 200 m cells near the camera and densely packed 800 m
cells for distant trees, grouped by variant with up to 500 nonuniform transforms per submission. Boughs and
sprays are drawn to 80 m (level 0) and 300 m (level 1); beyond that a tree is its crown volumes (level 2), and
from 1200 m a single low shape (level 3). Detail is chosen per submission from its distance. Bounds contain the
transformed mesh at every level plus wind padding. A tree's own mesh casts its sun shadow to 110 m; beyond that
each 800 m cell's static shadow-only mesh takes over: one ten-triangle double pyramid per tree, shaped from
`TreeVariant.crown` and drawn by `TreeShadow.shader` out to 2600 m. There are no individual tree GameObjects or copied tree
meshes in the valley. The distant transform/index buffers cost an extra 68 bytes per surviving tree (34 MB for
500,000 trees); they keep overview submission counts small while fine cells cull expensive branches close to
the camera. Clearing compacts only the affected fine buffers and their parent buffers, preserving all other
arrays and surviving transforms. Local grading reseats both detail levels using displayed terrain heights. A
changed world or appended population rebuilds the hierarchy. `LastSubmittedTrees`, the detail counts
(`LastImpostorTrees` = trees drawn as volumes only), `LastSubmittedTriangles`, `LastDrawCalls` and
`LastRemovalCheckedTrees` expose camera and clearing work for the visual/performance review tools.

The conifer bough atlas is an AI-generated transparent branchlet atlas, not a botanical scan. The original
1254 × 1254 RGBA PNG and exact generation prompt are preserved in [ArtSource/World](../../ArtSource/World/README.md).
Fir/Spruce/Pine are art categories here, rather than a claim of accurate species simulation.
The leaf-spray atlas, palm fronds and every crown shape are generated by the runtime palette; they do not
add another downloaded or AI-generated texture.

Regenerate with **AirCity > World > Regenerate Trees and Public Road Assets**. This updates existing mesh
subassets without changing their IDs and rebakes distance foliage. `TreeLibrary.generatorVersion` is checked
by setup (bump the comparison/assigned version in setup when changing generation).
**AirCity > World > Create Tree and Road Review Scene** creates a separate scene containing the two species
and public-road kit. Batch entry `WorldAssetReview.FromCommandLine` writes close-up and airport forest captures
under ignored `Captures/WorldAssets`. `PublicRoadTests` checks the shared model/LOD/material contract.

`ForestDensityReview.RunFromCommandLine` captures each curated biome at fixed cameras and measures submitted
geometry and real brush work. The 2026-11-14 isolated Metal review matched all 13 camera pairs: Woodland
120,000 → 322,785 trees, Mountains 37,925 → 129,835, Tropical 18,561 → 32,922. Woodland's submitted triangles
fell 55% at the medium camera and 42% close up despite the denser forest. A 60 m clearing brush removed
102 trees in 1.09 ms plus 5.11 ms for the local visual refresh (17.78 ms initial index creation measured
separately). These are editor CPU/submission measurements, not frame-rate promises. The initial full
forest buffer build cost 469 ms and distant buffers consume additional memory; clearing uses the local path.
Matched previews and measurements live under ignored `Captures/NewGame/ForestDensity/{before,after}` and
`Logs/forest-density-{before,after}.json`. `ForestPopulationTests`, `ForestQueryTests`, `ForestRendererTests`
and the dense save regression protect placement, saved clearing, selection and both instance levels.
The combined isolated Game/World EditMode run passed 176/176 tests (104.83 s, no failures or skips;
`Logs/forest-tests.xml`).

The subsequent biome/depth review increased Bora Bora from 32,922 to 104,725 trees, including a grove on
the small 33-source-cell reef island that previously held only two trees. Woodland retains its 322,785
saved roots while gaining the mixed palette. Shared ground scenery now includes clustered outcrops,
stone aprons, shrubs, larger ferns and fallen timber; the common near tree meshes and distance thresholds
are deliberately cheaper to leave room for those landscape layers. The isolated Game/World regression
suite passed 187/187 tests in 103.17 s with no failures or skips (`Logs/biome-tests.xml`), including tropical
version 1 migration, dry low-island roots, a uniform bounded forest budget, all palette geometry/material
contracts, full prop clearances, local clearing/terrain updates and save/load. Matched review images live
under `Captures/NewGame/ForestDensity/biome-{before,after}`.
The final Metal review passed 19 matched camera pairs plus two ground-feature showcases. At the normal
Woodland camera, trees plus visible props submitted 452,358 triangles, compared with 4,171,126 triangles
for trees alone before this pass; Bora submitted 309,960 combined versus 424,964 for the previous trees
alone. At the close Woodland camera the combined total was 5,595,514 versus 11,743,992 previous tree-only
triangles. Earlier ground geometry was not instrumented, so those baselines are conservative lower bounds,
not exact combined comparisons or frame-rate measurements. See `Logs/biome-visual-review.txt`.

`VegetationFrameTimeReview.RunFromCommandLine` measures frame time rather than submitted geometry: fixed
cameras in Woodland and Tropical, rendered at 1920 × 1080 with trees, ground cover and sun shadows switched
on and off (`Logs/vegetation-frame-time.txt`); `-vegetationReviewBase` splits the scene without vegetation
into terrain, water and fixed passes (`Logs/base-frame-time.txt`). On 2026-11-19 (Apple M2, Metal, editor
batch mode: compare the parts, not the totals) the terrain alone took 11–23 ms in Woodland and 19–46 ms in
Tropical, water at most 1.6 ms. The two runs agreed within 3% in Woodland, but Tropical's scene without
vegetation came out up to 38% slower in the second, so its figures are rough. Trees took 25 ms at the
Woodland region overview, 18 ms of it on the main thread submitting 279,338 transforms and unchanged at a
quarter of the pixels. At 70–145 m trees took 19–23 ms in Woodland and 28–34 ms in Tropical, more than half
of it their sun shadows (12–21 ms); ground cover took 2–7 ms there and under 2.5 ms farther out. The first
figures are kept in `Logs/*-before.txt`.

After the solid-shape trees and the terrain sampling change (same day, same cameras), the whole frame fell
from 48 to 41 ms at the Woodland region overview, 41 to 28 ms at 145 m and 56 to 34 ms at 70 m; in Tropical
from 34 to 29 ms, 86 to 38 ms and 93 to 44 ms. Trees at 70–145 m now take 11–13 ms in Woodland and 7–11 ms in
Tropical, their shadows 4–7 ms. The region overview's tree cost barely moved (25 to 21 ms): it is the main
thread re-sending every transform each frame, which only GPU-resident instance buffers would remove.

Remaining visual work includes closer bark detail, wind-driven branch motion, species/age distributions,
slope/root treatment and blended transitions between levels.

Reference: [Unity RenderMeshInstanced API](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.6/script-reference/unityengine/graphics/rendermeshinstanced)
for the shared-instance rendering approach; [RHS Norway spruce](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/12899/picea-abies/details)
for the broad conifer silhouette direction.
